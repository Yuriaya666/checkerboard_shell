# Actual-units aircraft morphing-skin study

## Scenario

- NACA 2412 camber line, aft 30% chord;
- chord `0.60 m`, span `0.30 m`;
- aluminium skin, `t = 2 mm`, `E = 73 GPa`, `nu = 0.33`;
- uniform pressure `5 kPa`;
- nine actuators at three rib stations (three chordwise points each);
- actuator limit `±500 N`;
- target: external `+5 mm` trailing-edge camber increase, not generated by the
  actuators.

The panel is the skin between the rear spar and the trailing edge.  The front
edge is clamped; the span edges are free in this representative panel model.

## Results

| method | iterations | work | total time | target error | max error | max force | free DOF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Checkerboard + adjoint** | 10 | **44 linear solves** | **0.19 s** | 3.63% | 0.250 mm | 70.6 N | **297** |
| MITC4 + finite difference | 19 | 401 analyses | 8.98 s | 3.55% | 0.203 mm | 59.0 N | 594 |

- All actuator forces are well inside `±500 N`; the residual is limited by the
  spatial distribution of nine actuators, not by the force bound.
- Target error is about `3.6%` of the target norm, with a maximum local error of
  `0.25 mm` on a `5 mm` target.
- The Checkerboard adjoint loop is about **48x** faster on this case.  This is
  an implementation-level comparison: MITC4 rebuilds the model for every
  finite-difference analysis; a MITC4 adjoint would reduce its work units.

## What this case is

It is a real-scale, external-target numerical study: NACA geometry, aluminium
skin, pressure load, actuator limits, and a prescribed camber target.  It is
not a full wing, not a composite layup, not a rib/skin contact model, and not
experimentally validated.
