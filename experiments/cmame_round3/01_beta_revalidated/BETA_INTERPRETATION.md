# Revalidated beta_M interpretation

## A. Is c_beta=1 a broad response platform?

Yes for the tested recovered manuscript observables.  The maximum relative
change from the `c_beta=1` response over `0.25 <= c_beta <= 4` is zero for the
plate scalar transverse branch, `1.63e-5` for roof n=16, and `4.21e-10` for
M3 n=12.  There is no sharp response optimum at one.

## B. How sensitive is normal load-bearing response?

Sensitivity is low on these three linear observables, but not identically zero
in general.  Including the removal case and `c_beta=8`, the largest change is
`1.36e-3` for the roof, `5.04e-10` for M3, and zero for the transverse plate
branch.  These values do not establish sensitivity for arbitrary nonlinear or
in-plane loading.

## C. Does beta_M mainly affect the target channel?

The completion-added `DX_M` directional stiffness is exactly linear in
`c_beta`.  At `c_beta=1`, its quadratic-energy fraction is `3.15e-7` on the
roof and `4.20e-24` on M3.  The data therefore show a strong separation
between the deliberately stiffened target channel and these normal responses.
The plate benchmark's scalar transverse branch contains no active membrane
completion channel.

## D. Does c_beta=0 expose the membrane defect?

Not as an additional exact null mode of the complete curved roof/M3 tangent
under their benchmark BC: their thresholded nullities remain unchanged.  The
same plate n=16 reference, restricted to the free flat in-plane membrane
operator, has nullity 6 at `c_beta=0` and 5 at `c_beta=1`.  The isolated extra
direction has base stiffness `9.62e-20` and completion-added stiffness
`8.27e-5`.  This supports precisely the one-dimensional *flat* quotient-defect
statement, not a general curved-reference nullity theorem.
