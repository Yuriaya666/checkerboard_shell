# Application studies: aircraft skin and automotive panel

Two force-inverse studies on curved thin panels.  Both use nine design forces,
the production Checkerboard adjoint, and a black-box MITC4 finite-difference
baseline.  The targets are numerical; the geometries are representative, not
proprietary CAD.

---

## A. Aircraft morphing-skin panel

### Geometry and loads

- Curved trailing-edge skin panel, `12x8` mesh;
- width `1.0`, height `0.6`, thickness `0.04`;
- `E=2.0e5`, `nu=0.3`;
- initial camber `0.06 sin(pi x / 2) [0.5 + 0.5 cos(pi(y-0.3)/0.6)]`;
- left two columns clamped; `297` free DOF.

Nine actuation forces in a `3x3` patch near the trailing edge:

```text
x = 0.75, 0.833, 0.917
y = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75
```

The target is a smooth camber-like displacement generated by a Gaussian force
patch centered at `(0.82, 0.30)`, `sigma_x=sigma_y=0.12`, total `0.02`.
The target is measured at 99 free nodes.

### Results

| method | iterations | work | optimization | total | target error | force error | free DOF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Checkerboard + adjoint | 13 | 68 linear solves | 0.26 s | **1.25 s** | **6.87e-5** | 0.172 | **297** |
| MITC4 + finite difference | 6 | 287 analyses | 6.21 s | 6.21 s | 1.12e-4 | 0.273 | 594 |

- Total-time ratio: about **5.0x** in favor of the Checkerboard adjoint loop (single run; timing varies).
- Work ratio: about **4.2x** fewer work units.
- The free state is about **2x** smaller (`297` vs `594`).
- Force error is not small; the force vector is not unique.

## B. Automotive panel fixture / gap control

### Geometry and loads

- Cylindrical panel, `12x8` mesh;
- width `1.0`, height `0.6`, thickness `0.02`;
- `E=2.0e5`, `nu=0.3`;
- initial curvature `0.04 sin(pi y)`;
- rear edge (bottom two rows) clamped; `273` free DOF.

Nine fixture forces in a `3x3` patch near the free top/right corner:

```text
x = 0.667, 0.833, 0.917
y = 0.30, 0.45, 0.60
```

The target is a gap/flush field at 11 measurement points: five on the right
edge and six on the rear half of the top edge.  It is generated by a Gaussian
force patch centered at `(0.90, 0.45)`, `sigma_x=sigma_y=0.12`, total `0.01`.

### Results

| method | iterations | work | optimization | total | target error | force error | free DOF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Checkerboard + adjoint | 24 | 89 linear solves | 0.33 s | **1.00 s** | **1.28e-4** | 0.068 | **273** |
| MITC4 + finite difference | 16 | 401 analyses | 7.91 s | 7.91 s | 2.55e-4 | 0.180 | 546 |

- Total-time ratio: about **7.9x** in favor of the Checkerboard adjoint loop (single run; timing varies).
- Work ratio: about **4.5x** fewer work units.
- The free state is about **2x** smaller (`273` vs `546`).
- Force error is small for Checkerboard but not unique.

---

## What the two cases show

1. **Force inverse on curved panels is feasible** with nine forces: target
   errors are `1e-4` or better for both the aircraft camber target and the
   automotive gap target.
2. **The adjoint design loop is cheaper** than a black-box MITC4 finite
   difference loop: about `5x` (aircraft) and `8x` (automotive) in total time,
   with about half the free DOF.
3. **The two cases are not identical**: the aircraft case uses a full-field
   camber target and a left-edge clamp; the automotive case uses a gap/flush
   subset and a rear-edge clamp.
4. **Forces are not unique**: the target shape can be reached by different
   force vectors.  Practical use needs actuator/fixture limits, placement, or
   energy constraints.

---

## Caveats

- Each model is asked to reproduce the target generated by **its own** `f_true`;
  the target errors are not an accuracy comparison between elements.
- MITC4 uses a black-box finite-difference loop because OpenSees does not expose
  an adjoint; a MITC4 adjoint would reduce its work units.  The comparison is
  implementation-level, not a pure element benchmark.
- The panels are isotropic, idealized, and free of contact, plasticity,
  composites, and thermal effects.  These are representative numerical
  studies, not experimental validation.
- No IGA baseline is run; IGA is only a conceptual relative in the
  geometry-driven design discussion.
